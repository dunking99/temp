Chip demand carries the tape while oil gives back the week's gains

By Meridian Markets Desk · updated 4 minutes ago

Equities are modestly higher in quiet trade, with the S&P 500 up 0.6% and the Nasdaq 100 adding 0.9% as semiconductor names extend their run: NVDA +1.8%, TSM +2.2%, AVGO +1.6%. The move comes ahead of TSMC results on Wednesday, which the options market is pricing for a ±4.2% swing.

The other side of the tape is energy. Brent is down 1.3% at $68.42 after a third consecutive crude inventory build, and the sector is the day's worst performer at −1.05%, with APA and HAL both printing fresh 52-week lows. Gold is doing the defensive work instead, up 0.8% at $3,142 while 10-year real yields hold near 1.72%.

Rates are quiet before Wednesday's CPI. The curve has steepened a further 2bp, taking 2s10s to +54bp — the widest since the un-inversion began in late 2024. Nothing in the tape suggests positioning ahead of the print is stretched either way.

Risk appetite is broadening at last. Advance/decline is running +18,420 and 84 stocks printed new 52-week highs against 31 lows — the best ratio since November. Critically, the leadership is widening beyond the megacap complex: industrials and utilities are both at the top of the sector table alongside technology.

The rates backdrop is the cleanest it has been in two years. A bull-steepening curve with the front end anchored is exactly the configuration that preceded the 1995 and 2013 advances, and credit agrees — IG spreads at 88bp and HY at 288bp are both in their tightest two deciles.

With CPI expected at +0.2% and the market holding $14bn of cash redemptions to put to work, the path of least resistance remains higher into Wednesday.

Beneath a quiet index the internals are deteriorating. The A/D line has flatlined for eleven sessions while the index ground to a record — narrower leadership than the last four corrections began with, and today's +0.6% is being carried by four names.

The bar into Wednesday's CPI is high. Options are pricing a 1.2% move either way and skew sits at its 78th percentile, so downside protection is expensive precisely because people are buying it. A core print at +0.3% would price a March cut out of the curve entirely.

Meanwhile the credit market has stopped confirming: IG at 88bp is 12th-percentile rich and the 30-year auction tailed 3.8bp last month. Two of the last three bond sales have been poorly received.
